# Voice playbook — Jesse ("Caught F*** All Fishing")

Derived from 7 auto-generated transcripts (~11,300 words of speech) in
`reference/transcripts/`, plus `titles.tsv`. Every rule below is grounded in that corpus.
Counts in **[brackets]** are raw occurrences across the 7 files, given so you can tell a
signature habit from a one-off.

**Corpus caveats — read before trusting a rule:**
- Transcripts are machine-generated. Swearing is masked (77 masked words, ~1 every 145
  words), and proper nouns are garbled (Guilderton → "Gildedon", Yanchep → "Yenship",
  the Aerokontiki drone → "the arrow"/"air route"). Treat spellings as unreliable; treat
  *frequencies* as reliable.
- All 7 videos are beach/drone fishing in coastal WA from what is plainly an early phase
  of the channel (references to "the first 20 videos", "since I started filming").
- **He does not land a big fish in any of these transcripts.** There is no evidence here
  for how he sounds during a genuine trophy catch. Do not invent that register — the
  biggest reactions in the corpus are for ~40cm pinkies. If a script needs a big-catch
  beat, flag it rather than guessing.
- Runtime can't be measured from text. Speech volume is 450–2,825 words per video; a lot
  of runtime is music, drone shots and waiting, so word count is not a proxy for length.
- No tutorial, review or talking-head video in the corpus — only field vlogs (the drone
  video is a short field-footage cut, not a review despite the title).

## Who he is / what the channel is

Perth-based drone fisherman, filming himself beach fishing along the WA coast — Yanchep,
Guilderton, Moore River, Jurien Bay, Kalbarri, Lancelin-way — flying baits 200–400 m out
to reef and waiting. Chasing pink snapper ("pinkies") and mulloway ("jew"); mostly
catching nothing, which is the actual premise of the channel and its name.

Format is a chronological day-in-the-field vlog: solo pieces to camera on the beach,
often joined by regular mates (Luke, Mark, Chris). Unscripted throughout. Sometimes a
single session, sometimes two days cut as one video.

He's talking to other WA shore/drone fishos — peers, not students. He assumes they know
the species, the gear and the local beaches, and he positions himself as a bloke still
figuring it out rather than an authority. Titles match the voice: anti-hype hype, built
on the failure (`Nothing Went Right…`, `DON'T WATCH THIS! – It's boring!`,
`Redemption FAIL at…`), sentence case, ellipses and em-dashes, occasional emoji.

## Structure

**Scripted or loose:** loose, always. Live present-tense commentary with no script and no
retakes. Sentences restart mid-thought, he corrects himself out loud, and he narrates
things as they happen rather than in hindsight. A script written for him must read like
someone talking while doing something else with their hands.

**The open** — a consistent four-beat pattern, all four in under ~150 words:
1. **Greeting.** "Good day" or "Good day, guys" [7], usually with "welcome back to another
   episode" [4]. Sometimes doubled ("Good day. Good day."). One variant riffs on the
   greeting instead of playing it straight — listing every time of day the viewer might
   be watching. One returning-from-a-break video opens with mock-accusation at the viewer
   before any greeting.
2. **Where we are, named specifically.** Actual place name plus a locating detail ("just
   south of Yanchep Lagoon", "just up in Guilderton"). Never a vague "out at the beach".
3. **The plan and the reasoning behind it.** What reef, how far out, why this spot — often
   sourced from someone ("a local tackle shop guy told us", "I've heard from a lot of
   fishermen"). Conditions get stated honestly here, good or bad.
4. **A pre-emptive hedge, then a launch line.** He undercuts his own plan before starting:
   the video won't exist if they catch nothing; it's probably not worth it; he'll probably
   just get snagged. Then a short kick-off: "Let's get into it, eh?" / "Let's get these
   baits out" / "we'll crack on".

Do **not** open with a teaser montage, a question to the viewer, or a promise of what's
coming. There is none in the corpus.

**The middle** — episodic, driven by drops, not by argument:
- The spine is numbered attempts: first drop → check the bait → second drop → third. He
  announces each one ("All righty, drop two"), and "All righty"/"All right" [20+] is the
  main scene-change marker. Retrieval is its own beat: what came back, what got eaten,
  what's gone.
- Between drops, live reaction commentary in short bursts — something's having a go at
  it, come on, go on, hook up. He talks directly *to* the fish, the ocean, the birds and
  the sandflies, in the imperative.
- Gear tangents drop in unannounced and end just as abruptly: bait cotton, hook sizes,
  rig-building, which bait holds up against pickers, the drone's GPS lid, line capacity,
  Navionics. These are *mate-showing-you* explanations, not lessons.
- Unpaid, unprompted recommendations by name — a sinker brand, a tackle shop that spools
  line for you. No sponsor framing, no disclosure, no "link in the description".
- Shout-outs to other fishing channels by name, sincerely and without envy.
- Genuine digressions: rock pools at low tide, a car stereo he installed himself, rubbish
  left at a campsite, seagulls fighting, a fire, food. He goes and looks at things because
  he's got time. These are not filler in his voice — they're the point of the day.
- Occasional bigger reflective monologue, still unscripted, usually after a bad session:
  the state of the channel, whether he's posting too often, why he keeps going.

**The close** — three beats, sometimes only two:
1. **Call it.** "Well, that's us" / "that's me for the day" / "Well, that's that."
2. **Tally the damage, then find the consolation.** Says plainly what went wrong and what
   he caught (usually nothing), then reframes: a day on the beach instead of at work, a
   good sunset, back tomorrow to give it another crack. Never ends bitter.
3. **Sign-off.** Short and flat: "Till next time" / "See you on the next one" / "See you,
   legends" / "Thanks for watching".

The standard like-comment-subscribe CTA appears **once** in 7 videos [3 total mentions of
"subscribe", one of which is thanking existing subscribers]. When it does appear it's
tacked on at the very end, rushed, and bundled into a shrug — "all that good stuff". Use
it sparingly and never at the top.

One video adds a post-outro bonus segment, openly acknowledging he already did the outro
and carrying on anyway. That move is in character.

## Sentence rhythm and speech habits

- **Short and fragmentary by default.** Most sentences are under 12 words. Longer ones
  arrive as run-ons stitched with "and"/"so"/"cuz", not as balanced clauses.
- **Drop the subject and the auxiliary.** "Got the boys over here." "Sent out some burley."
  "Doesn't get much better." "Bit knackered." This is the single most transferable habit —
  a script that keeps every "I have" and "It is" will not sound like him.
- **First-person plural for himself.** He says *we* while fishing completely alone [we ×204
  vs I ×411] — we're snagged, we've lost the rig, we're back in the car. He switches to
  **I** for confessions, opinions and channel talk. Follow that split; it does real work.
- **Sentence openers, in order of frequency:** "So" [66], "Oh" [84], "Well" [21],
  "All righty"/"All right" [20], "Yeah", "But", "Uh" [53], "Anyway" [5], "Look",
  "Hopefully" [16], "I reckon" [10].
- **Tag questions everywhere.** "eh?" [11] is the signature — attached to statements to
  invite agreement. "hey" is used the same way as a *sentence-final tag* [4], not a
  greeting: "it seems darker, hey". Also "right?", "can we?", "doesn't it?".
- **Keep the fillers.** "uh" [53], "um" [12], "oh" [84], "like" [91, mostly as a hedge or
  approximator], "you know" [14], "I mean", "kind of", "obviously", "basically". These are
  not noise to be cleaned up — strip them and the voice dies. Target roughly one hedge or
  filler every 25–30 words.
- **Contractions always**, including the sloppy ones. Never write out "it is", "we are",
  "going to" as separate formal words when speech would contract them.
- **"me" for "my"** in casual possessives — me bait, me mic, me keys, me luck [8]. Not
  every time; it surfaces when he's rushed or annoyed.
- **Tense:** present-progressive live commentary as the default. Past only for recapping
  something off-camera. Future via "we'll" and "we're going to" [78] — never "I shall",
  never "I will".
- **Repetition for emphasis** rather than stronger words: "come on, come on", "very, very
  early", "more videos, more videos, more videos", "wave after wave after wave".
- **Real numbers, constantly.** Metres out (200/300/350/500/810), degrees (3°, 17°, 19°),
  fish length in centimetres (37, 45, "might be a nice 50"), line rating (65 lb), hook
  sizes (8/0), dollars ("about four bucks each", "100 bucks later"). Specificity is how he
  builds credibility — he never says "a long way out" when he knows the number.

## Vocabulary and register

**Formality: none.** Broad, unpolished Australian spoken English. No sentence in the
corpus would survive a copyeditor, and that's correct.

**Swearing is core, not garnish.** ~1 masked swear every 145 words, and the channel name
itself is built on one. It functions as punctuation and as intensifier, mostly at
frustration points, and it's affectionate as often as it's angry. Write it in.

**"Bloody" is the signature intensifier** [45] — bloody long time, bloody handy, bloody
nice, bloody mission, bloody hell. Also "absolute/absolutely" [13] for both good and bad
(absolute monster, absolutely smoked, absolute cracking sunset).

**Australian usage actually present in the corpus** — use these, don't extrapolate beyond
them: g'day/"good day", mate [19], bloke, legends (as address), reckon [10], heaps [5],
chuck, unreal [8], mint [3], sick [9], epic, flash, sketchy, dodgy, knackered, tight-ass,
far out, no dramas, cop it on the chin, first cab off the rank, each to their own, risk it
for the biscuit, gives me the shits, punching on, smashed, help a bloke out, cheers, take
it easy, do it for the lols. "bro" appears too [12], used to mates and to the ocean.

**"Crack" is his most-loaded word** [16] and carries three senses — give it a crack
(attempt), having a crack at it (a fish biting), and a cracking day (excellent). All three
are in play; use them all.

**Fishing terms he uses with NO explanation** (assume the audience knows): pinky/pinkies,
jew/jewie, flatty, herring, mullet, muies, scalies, tailor, rock cod, dart, wrasse,
pickers, burley, bait cotton, gang hooks, grapnel sinker, rig, leader, drop, soak, snagged,
busted off, spooled, weed, swell, brag mat, BCF, Anaconda, Navionics.

**What he does explain**, and how: regulations and general knowledge get flagged with
"If anyone doesn't know…" / "For anyone that doesn't know…" — e.g. that you can't fly a
drone at night. Technique gets explained in second-person procedural form, mid-task, as if
showing you over his shoulder: what you want to do is measure so the last hook sits near
the eye. He explains *because it just came up*, never as a titled segment, and never
patronisingly. Species names are never glossed.

## Humour and attitude

**The self-deprecating pattern is the engine of the channel.** It runs in a repeatable
three-step shape, and it should be built into scripts deliberately:

1. **Predict his own failure before it happens.** He announces the likely stuff-up in
   advance — he'll probably get snagged and lose the whole rig; it's guaranteed not to
   rain, which would be unusual for his trips; hopefully it's worth it, but probably not.
2. **Let it land, and react flat.** When it goes wrong the reaction is deadpan and short —
   "Excellent." "Unreal." "Well, that's that." Note that **"unreal" is sincere for good
   things and sarcastic for bad ones**, and the flip is never signposted.
3. **Own it as a running bit.** He keeps a live tally of his own incompetence — forgot the
   chest harness one day, forgot the mic the next, "it's not one thing, it's another".
   He explicitly counts the stuff-ups on the channel. Gear failures get the same
   treatment: three drone crashes in six months, reported plainly, with disappointment
   rather than a rant.

**Superstition as a bit.** A recurring gag where saying a thing out loud causes it: the
rod goes off the moment he mentions it's gone quiet, the battery dies as soon as he counts
on it. He points out that this happens in every video — the joke is that he knows.

**On failure generally:** honest, never falsely upbeat, never self-pitying for long. He
states the loss precisely (two rigs, 200 m of line, ten hours over two days, no fish) and
then lands on a consolation that's genuinely felt — a day on the beach beats a day at
work, the sunset was worth it, back tomorrow. He explicitly says the channel shows the
realities of fishing rather than the highlights, and he treats the empty days as the
honest content, not as an apology.

**He undersells his catches.** A small fish is called small, held up and mocked, measured,
and joked about — hold it closer to the camera and stretch your arm out. There is no
manufactured hype when something is landed. Catches get released with a line like
"something's always better than nothing".

**With the audience:** peer to peer. He calls them guys, legends, mate, bro. He's openly
surprised anyone watches, thanks subscribers and commenters sincerely, and has a whole
segment praising the Perth fishing YouTube community for being generous — explicitly
contrasting it with a photography Instagram audience that treated him as competition. He
asks the audience for help without pretence (anyone want to share their honey holes) and
is transparent about the channel's problems: the drought, the pressure filming adds, going
out four times a week, whether that's sustainable. He also cheerfully insults them and
himself in the same breath — telling them they'll keep watching whether he catches
anything or not, or that watching him is epic because he's a good-looking bloke.

## What he never does

Every item here is an **absence** verified across all 7 transcripts.

- **Never uses American YouTube register.** No "what's up guys", "hey everyone", "welcome
  back to the channel", "let's dive in", "without further ado", "let's get right into it".
  His welcome is "Good day" + "welcome back to another episode" — keep that exact register.
- **Never front-loads a CTA.** No like/subscribe/notification-bell ask in the intro, ever.
  No "before we get started, make sure to…". The only CTA is flat and at the very end.
- **Never opens with a hook montage or a teaser** of a fish he catches later. No
  "you won't believe what happened". No cold-open cliffhanger.
- **Never promises an outcome.** He hedges toward failure instead. No "today we're going
  to smash them", no "this spot never disappoints".
- **Never claims expertise.** No "pro tip", no "the secret is", no numbered listicles, no
  "here's what most people get wrong". He says outright that he doesn't know what he's
  doing and that other people make it look easier.
- **Never fakes enthusiasm about a small fish**, and never pretends a bad day was good.
- **No sponsor reads, no affiliate framing, no "link in the description".** Product
  mentions are spontaneous and unpaid.
- **No corporate or written-essay vocabulary.** Absent entirely: furthermore, however,
  ultimately, essentially(as connective), game-changer, level up, unlock, leverage, epic
  fail(as a phrase), "in this video we'll". If a sentence would look at home in a blog
  post, it's wrong.
- **No motivational or inspirational sign-off.** No life lessons, no "chase your dreams".
  The closest he gets is hoping his early failures might encourage someone else to keep
  going — and even that is delivered offhand, mid-ramble.
- **No moralising** about other fishos, brands or people. Even the campsite litterers get
  a shrug and a joke, not a lecture.
- **No apology for the video being boring.** He names it and leans into it — one title is
  literally an instruction not to watch — but he doesn't grovel.
- **No polished transitions.** No "meanwhile", no "fast forward", no "so let's talk about".
  Scene changes are "All righty" or nothing at all.
- **No explaining local species or slang** to an outside audience.
- **Leans Australian over American** on nearly every lexical choice — mate not dude/buddy,
  bloody not freaking, heaps not tons, arvo-adjacent time words over "PM". (Honest
  exception: "tons" does appear once, so this is a strong tendency, not an absolute.)

## Writing for him: hard rules

A script has to pass all of these:

1. **Open with the four beats:** "Good day" greeting → named specific location → the plan
   and why → a hedge that predicts failure → short launch line. No teaser, no CTA.
2. **Structure the body around numbered attempts and what came back**, not around topics
   or an argument. Mark each transition with "All righty"/"All right".
3. **Plant the failure before it happens**, then react to it deadpan and short. At least
   one self-deprecating callback to his own incompetence per script.
4. **Say "we" while alone; switch to "I" for confessions and opinions.**
5. **Drop subjects and auxiliaries** in at least a third of sentences. Keep average
   sentence length under ~12 words.
6. **Keep the fillers** — roughly one "uh/oh/like/you know/I reckon" every 25–30 words.
   Never clean up a false start; write at least one self-correction in.
7. **Use "bloody" as the default intensifier**, and swear at genuine frustration points.
   Sanitised = off-voice.
8. **Land at least one "eh?" or sentence-final "hey"** as a tag question.
9. **Use real numbers** — metres, degrees, centimetres, pound rating, dollars. Never a
   vague quantity where he'd know the figure.
10. **Fishing terms unglossed; regulations and technique flagged with "if anyone doesn't
    know"** and explained second-person, mid-task.
11. **Include at least one unprompted digression** — scenery, gear, food, wildlife, rubbish
    on the beach — that goes nowhere.
12. **Talk directly to the fish, the ocean or the birds** at least once, in the imperative.
13. **Close by naming what went wrong, then finding the consolation**, then a short flat
    sign-off. Never end bitter, never end with a life lesson.
14. **If the script needs him to land a big fish, flag it** — the corpus has no evidence
    for that register. Don't invent it.
